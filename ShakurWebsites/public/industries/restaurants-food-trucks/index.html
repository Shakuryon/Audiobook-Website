Where Could You Be Losing Opportunities?

A customer discovers your food on social media or Google, but then has to search for an up-to-date menu, today's location, current hours, catering details, or a way to order.

Potential Revenue Leaks

  • Outdated menus, hours, or truck locations create uncertainty before a customer visits.
  • Customers may abandon the search when ordering or catering information is hard to find.
  • Third-party platforms can introduce fees and place another step between your brand and the customer.

What Your Website Can Do

  • Mobile-first menus with clear categories, prices, photos, and dietary notes supplied by you.
  • Prominent order, call, directions, and catering inquiry links.
  • Food truck location and event schedule sections that are easy to update.
  • Search-friendly pages for your cuisine, location, catering, and signature offerings.

More Than a Website. A Better Customer Journey.

A website can link to your existing ordering provider or support a suitable direct-ordering integration. Payment processing, delivery logistics, and third-party platform fees depend on the provider you choose.

The goal is not to abandon delivery apps. It is to give customers another clear route to your menu, your brand, catering, and repeat business.

What the Industry Signals Tell Us

National Restaurant Association’s 2025 off-premises report found that 47% of U.S. adults picked up takeout at least weekly and 37% ordered delivery weekly. Popmenu’s 2026 consumer research reports that 94% of surveyed guests prefer ordering directly from a restaurant’s own website. These findings support offering a clear direct-ordering route alongside third-party discovery platforms; they do not predict results for an individual restaurant.
